## Project substance and technical depth

The group must have five students. The course is a thinking and writing project, and working software is optional and not graded. The central requirements are a specific original question, careful investigation, a research gap supported by related work, appropriate computing or data science reasoning, and a clear write-up (general guidelines pages 1-4).

The chosen main type is data analysis, with a supporting interface demonstration. For data-analysis projects, the general guidelines page 7 require a clear account of data types, sources, collection or acquisition, and relevance to the research question. There must be concrete analytical thinking: examples include descriptive statistics, visualisations, and basic modelling or rule-based classification. The guidelines allow conceptual or toy-data analysis at course level, but this submitted plan voluntarily commits to a real 20-branch pilot, a 100-branch study, and independently reviewed validation pairs. Any achieved-work claim must therefore distinguish the course minimum from these additional project promises.

For this project, appropriate evidence comprises an actual dated sampling frame, documented inclusion and branch-deduplication rules, source extracts, matching and normalisation rules, manually reviewed match examples, explicit coverage and agreement denominators, figures, limitations, and a clear explanation of what differences can and cannot show. A screenshot or functioning website alone does not establish the promised investigation.

## Independent work and authentic contribution

The guidelines require each member to make a fair contribution. The peer-review form flags potential free-rider cases but does not directly adjust scores; any adjustment follows an investigation (general guidelines pages 5-6). Do not manufacture contribution histories, hours, meetings, human labels, completed peer reviews, personal learning, or personal reflections. A computer-prepared log or reflection can only be a template until the named student supplies and verifies their own experience.

Two independent human reviewers and an adjudicator are part of the submitted validation plan. Machine-suggested labels can support review but cannot be described as those independent human judgements. Precision, recall and F1 must remain unavailable until the labelled evaluation data and relevant denominators actually exist.
